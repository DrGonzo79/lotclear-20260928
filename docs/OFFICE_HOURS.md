# Office Hours — LotClear

Generated 2026-09-28 · Startup diagnostic · Pre-product

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** demand is not verified. A rejected title plausibly delays lender funding and inventory resale, but only paid dealer pilots count. Newsletter interest does not.
2. **Painful status quo:** at a 10–75-car independent lot, the office manager assembles paper deal jackets, searches state PDFs, calls the DMV or an outside title runner, submits, then discovers a missing signature or form weeks later. One reject can strand $20k–$40k of cash and consume repeated follow-up.
3. **Desperate specificity:** start with the title clerk or office manager at a multi-state independent used-car dealer handling 40–200 retail transactions/month without a veteran in-house title specialist. They are measured on time-to-funding and clean-title aging; repeated rejects threaten both margin and trust.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** a human-verified preflight check for one outbound title packet in Florida, Georgia, or North Carolina, returned with a named missing-item checklist. Charge per clean packet, not for a generic “AI agent.”
5. **Observation plan:** sit beside five clerks for ten live deal jackets without guiding them. Record where they leave the DMS, which forms they recheck, and every correction. Offer the next 20 preflights at $49 each; success is two dealers paying and a measured first-pass acceptance lift.
6. **Future-fit:** state digitization increases structured access but not uniformity. A versioned rules-and-evidence layer becomes more essential as workflows move online; direct DMV portals could reduce the wedge if they ship strong preflight validation themselves.

## Premise challenge

- The customer buys fewer rejected packets and faster cash, not AI.
- Filing and “chasing every rejection” are regulated, state-specific service work. The first wedge should preflight and route to a qualified human, not promise autonomous submission.
- A static rule list becomes dangerous when stale; every production rule needs jurisdiction, effective date, source citation, reviewer and version.

## Approaches considered

### A — Human-verified preflight (minimal viable)
Effort S · Risk Low. A clerk uploads/redacts a packet and receives a checklist reviewed by a title specialist. Fastest willingness-to-pay test; operationally manual.

### B — Rules-backed title desk (recommended)
Effort M · Risk Medium. Deterministic state rules, evidence per requirement, an exception queue and human approval. Repeatable and auditable without overclaiming autonomy.

### C — Multi-state filing network (ideal architecture)
Effort XL · Risk High. Direct portal integrations, credentialed agents, rejection management, payments and compliance operations. Valuable destination, premature before state access and liability are proven.

## Recommendation and assignment

Prototype B while selling A manually. Within seven days, recruit five independent-dealer office managers, observe ten packet builds, and ask two to pay $49 for a same-day preflight. Track first-pass acceptance, days-to-funding and correction minutes.
